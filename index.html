First time? Scroll down to "Customize Your Program" and select your age group and season phase. The program will automatically adjust for you.
Program Goal: Build hockey-ready athletes year-round with cyclable training phases of structured off-ice training.
How it works: Three progressive 4-week phases. Foundation builds movement quality and clean technique. Development adds strength, volume, and advanced skills. Performance converts everything into game-speed power.
Why it works: Every session pairs strength or conditioning with hockey-specific skills — stickhandling and shooting are integrated into every training day so the gains transfer directly to the ice. Volume and intensity progress week by week to drive real adaptation. Customizable by age, available days, and goals so each player trains at the right level.
Off-Season Program
Maximum Development
6 days/week · Full volume
Strength Building Power Development High Volume
This is when you make the biggest gains. Train 6 days/week with full volumes. Progress through all 12 weeks, then restart the cycle. Use the heavier weights and push intensity in Weeks 9-12.
Pre-Season Program
Intensity Ramp
5 days/week · Peak preparation
Game Speed Conditioning Peak Power Focus
4-6 weeks before season starts, ramp up intensity. Train 5 days/week. Focus on Weeks 9-12 exercises (performance phase) to peak your power and conditioning right before games begin.
In-Season Program
Maintenance
3 days/week · Reduced volume
Strength Maintenance Injury Prevention Recovery Focus
During games, train 3 days/week (Mon/Thu/Sat recommended). All volumes automatically reduce by 1 set. Use Weeks 1-4 exercises only. Goal: maintain strength without adding fatigue.

Fuel Like a Tiger

What you eat and drink directly impacts performance, recovery, and energy. Guidelines built for youth hockey players ages 8–18 training through a 12-week program.

Daily Hydration
How Much Water Per Day
Ages 8–12: Target 6–8 cups (48–64 oz) daily + more on training days
💧💧💧💧💧💧💧
Ages 13–18: Target 8–12 cups (64–96 oz) daily + more on training days
💧💧💧💧💧💧💧💧💧💧
Morning
Drink 1–2 cups immediately upon waking before anything else.
Before Training
Drink 1–2 cups 30–60 minutes before any workout.
Meal Timing Around Training
2–3 hrs before
Full meal — carbs + protein + some fat. Pasta with chicken, rice and eggs, turkey sandwich.
30–60 min before
Light snack only — banana, apple with peanut butter, yogurt, granola bar.
Within 30 min after
Recovery snack — protein + carbs. Chocolate milk, Greek yogurt with fruit, peanut butter toast.
1–2 hrs after
Full recovery meal — protein + carbs + vegetables. Chicken with rice, pasta with meat sauce.
What to Eat
🍞Carbohydrates — Primary Fuel
Whole grain bread, pasta, rice — slow-burning energy
Oatmeal — excellent pre-training breakfast
Fruits — bananas, apples, berries. Natural quick energy
Aim for 50–60% of daily calories from carbs on training days
🍗Protein — Build & Repair
Chicken, turkey, lean beef — complete protein
Eggs — best protein source, great any time of day
Greek yogurt, cottage cheese — high protein + calcium
Target 0.6–0.8g per lb bodyweight on training days
Avoid
Energy Drinks
Not for youth athletes. Harmful to developing hearts.
Soda / Pop / Candy
Empty sugar, no nutrition. Spikes energy then crashes it.
Fast Food on Game Days
High fat, high sodium. Avoid within 3 hrs of training.
Skipping Meals
Especially breakfast on training days. You can't train hard on an empty tank.
✅Great Snacks
Apple or banana with peanut butter
Greek yogurt with granola or berries
Cheese and whole grain crackers
Hard-boiled eggs with fruit
Chocolate milk — best post-workout recovery drink
Sleep
Sleep Goals for Youth Athletes
Ages 8–12
Target 9–11 hours per night. Growth hormone releases during deep sleep — this is when muscles repair and grow.
Ages 13–18
Target 8–10 hours per night. Skimping on sleep kills performance gains more than skipping a workout.
Screen-Free Wind Down
No screens 30–60 min before bed. Blue light disrupts sleep hormones.
Consistent Schedule
Same bedtime and wake time even on weekends. Your body performs best on a rhythm.

Drill Reference — With Diagrams
Side-to-Side Soft Touch
Beginner · Foundation
3×60 sec
Stand in hockey stance. Alternate tapping puck forehand to backhand in a steady rhythm. Eyes up. Soft hands, wrist roll on every touch.
▸ Cup the puck, don't slap it
Figure 8 — Horizontal
Beginner · Wrist Control
3×60 sec
Place two pucks shoulder-width apart. Stickhandle in a figure-8 around them using forehand and backhand.
▸ Smooth — let the puck flow around each puck
Figure 8 — Vertical
Beginner · Reach Extension
3×60 sec
Place two pucks vertically in front of you — one near, one far. Figure-8 around them. Builds reach and arm extension.
▸ Reach fully on the far puck
Around the World
Beginner · 360° Control
3×60 sec
Stickhandle puck in a full 360° circle around your body — forehand, behind, backhand, and around the front.
▸ Eyes up — feel the puck around
Puck Protection
Intermediate · Shielding
3×45 sec
Keep the puck behind your body, away from the imaginary defender in front of you. Stickhandle in tight space — small, controlled touches. Use your body to shield. Move side to side and pivot.
▸ Body between defender and puck
Triangle Drill
Intermediate · Direction Change
4×30 sec
3 markers in a triangle. Stickhandle puck around them in a figure-8 pattern. Forces tight turns and quick transitions both directions.
▸ Tight turns at each marker
Cone Weave
Intermediate · Speed + Control
5 runs timed
6 cones 2 feet apart. Weave through as fast as possible. Forehand-first then backhand-first. Time every run.
▸ Head up — feel the puck
Top Hand Only
Beginner · Wrist Strength
3×30 sec
Remove bottom hand. Dribble puck side-to-side using only the top hand. Builds the wrist strength behind all stickhandling.
▸ Bottom hand completely off the stick
Hand Switches
Intermediate · Grip Control
3×30 sec
Stand in hockey stance. Rapidly switch grip from forehand to backhand and back — moving the puck side-to-side as you switch. Builds wrist agility and hand coordination. Start slow, focus on clean transitions.
▸ Clean transitions — no fumbles
Wide Dribble
Beginner-Int · Reach
3×45 sec
Pull puck as wide as possible to each side. Full arm extension on both forehand and backhand. Critical for protecting the puck.
▸ Full arm extension on both sides
Speed Taps (30s)
All Levels · Baseline Test
4×30 sec
Maximum forehand/backhand taps in 30 seconds. Count every touch. Your baseline metric — track improvement over 12 weeks.
▸ Count every touch — log below
Stickhandling Under Fatigue
Advanced · Game Sim
4 rounds
10 burpees, then immediately 60 sec stickhandling. No rest. Simulates late-shift puck control.
▸ Maintain form when breathing hard
